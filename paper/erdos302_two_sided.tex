\author{Dmitry Khanukov}

\begin{abstract}
Let $f(N)$ denote the largest size of a subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$
containing no distinct $a,b,c$ with $1/a=1/b+1/c$.  A hierarchical exact
rational certificate on the nontrivial divisors of $139708800$ gives
\[
  \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N
  \leq\frac{140803024}{163562355}
  \approx 0.860852266403232.
\]
The upper finite certificate is checked by a dependency-free exact
verification path.  We also describe a derived lower-bound route, adapting a
structured positive-density construction developed by Della Pietra for
Erd\H{o}s Problem 301.  Its Lean integration through pinned external Problem
301/327 dependencies is in progress, so this draft does not claim the lower
conclusion.  The upper asymptotic proof is not formalized end to end in Lean.
This is partial progress and does not solve Problem 302.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and prior context}

Erd\H{o}s and Graham asked for the asymptotic size of a largest set avoiding
the displayed two-tail reciprocal relation \cite{ErdosGraham1980,Erdos302}.
The elementary interval constructions lead to the density $5/8$ lower
baseline discussed by Cambie, while van Doorn's disjoint-dilate argument gives
a $9/10$ upper baseline; both are recorded in the public problem discussion
\cite{Cambie302,VanDoorn302}.  The present draft improves the upper side and
records a release-gated route on the lower side; it does not prove that
$f(N)/N$ converges.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:upper}
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N
 \leq\frac{140803024}{163562355}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\section{A derived lower-bound route (integration in progress)}

This section records the candidate route without promoting its conclusion to
a theorem of the present draft.  The route is conditional on the following
structured input from Della Pietra's pinned Problem 301 development
\cite{DellaPietra301}; the repository's exact-pins Lean gate must compile this
interface and reproduce its axiom report before the lower conclusion is
released.  Put
\[
 P_L=\prod_{p<L}p,
 \qquad
 \rho_L=\frac{\varphi(P_L)}{P_L}
       =\prod_{p<L}\left(1-\frac1p\right).
\]
The product includes the prime $2$.

\begin{proposition}[structured input for the candidate route]\label{prop:structured}
For some fixed $L$, some fixed auxiliary parameters, and all sufficiently
large $N$, there is a set $C_N\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $C_N$ contains no all-distinct reciprocal relation of any tail length
      at least two;
\item every $n\in C_N$ satisfies either
\[
 N<3n,\qquad 2n<N,\qquad n\ \text{odd},
\]
or $N\leq2n$;
\item
\[
 |C_N|\geq\left(\frac12+\frac{\rho_L}{24}\right)N.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The exact Lean interfaces and immutable dependency revisions used for this
proposition are recorded in the repository.  The underlying analytic work
uses the centered-tail library developed for Problem 327
\cite{DellaPietra327}, whose human manuscript in turn invokes standard
analytic estimates \cite{Tenenbaum2015,BretecheTenenbaum2026}.  These external
developments are unrefereed.

Define the odd quarter
\[
 O_N=\{n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}: n\text{ is odd and }4n\leq N\},
 \qquad A_N=C_N\cup O_N.
\]

\begin{lemma}[odd-quarter padding]\label{lem:padding}
The set $A_N$ contains no distinct $a,b,c$ with
$1/a=1/b+1/c$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume such a triple exists and relabel the two tails so that $b<c$.
Every reciprocal relation has $a<b<c$.  Moreover $b<c$ gives
$1/a=1/b+1/c>2/c$, so $2a<c\leq N$.

Suppose first that $a\in C_N$.  The top-half alternative is impossible, so
$N<3a$.  Since $b,c>a>N/3>N/4$, neither tail lies in $O_N$.  Thus all three
denominators lie in $C_N$, contradicting its Problem 301 admissibility.

Now suppose that $a\in O_N$.  The head is odd.  From
\[
 bc=a(b+c)
\]
both tails are even: two odd tails make the two sides have opposite parity,
as does one odd and one even tail.  Therefore $b,c\notin O_N$ and both tails
belong to $C_N$.  Their low-band alternative is odd, so both lie in the top
half:
\[
 N\leq2b,\qquad N\leq2c.
\]
Since $b\ne c$, the two tails cannot both equal $N/2$.  Thus at least one of
the reciprocal inequalities $1/b\leq2/N$ and $1/c\leq2/N$ is strict, and
hence
\[
 \frac1b+\frac1c<\frac4N.
\]
On the other hand $4a\leq N$ gives $1/a\geq4/N$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The map $j\mapsto2j+1$ embeds $\{0,\ldots,\lfloor N/8\rfloor-1\}$ into
$O_N$, so
\[
 |O_N|\geq\lfloor N/8\rfloor\geq N/8-1.
\]
The two sets are disjoint: every element of $O_N$ is at most $N/4$, whereas
each alternative in Proposition~\ref{prop:structured} lies above $N/3$.
Consequently
\[
 |A_N|\geq
 \left(\frac58+\frac{\rho_L}{24}\right)N-1.
\]
Conditional on Proposition~\ref{prop:structured}, since $\rho_L>0$,
increasing $N_0$ absorbs the additive loss and would give the candidate lower
conclusion with
\[
 \delta=\frac{\rho_L}{48}>0.
\]
No numerical value of $L$ or $\delta$ is asserted.  This conclusion is not
part of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} in the present draft: exact-pins Lean
integration remains its release gate.

\section{The hierarchical finite certificate}

Let
\[
 Q=2^7 3^4 5^2 7^2 11=139708800,
 \qquad D=\operatorname{Div}(Q)\setminus\{1\}.
\]
The reciprocal-triple hypergraph $H$ has vertex set $D$ and one edge for each
unordered all-distinct triple satisfying $bc=a(b+c)$.  The verifier
regenerates $719$ vertices and $12675$ edges from these definitions.

We use a family $\mathcal G$ of configurations.  Every edge is a
configuration of demand one.  The remaining configurations are all $96$
admissible exponent shifts of each of the $21$ exactly verified prefixes of
the divisor tile
\[
 Q_0=3360=2^5\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7.
\]
Thus there are $96\cdot21=2016$ hierarchical gadgets and $14691$
configurations in total.  A gadget's demand is its exact base-prefix cover
number.

\begin{lemma}[configuration packing]\label{lem:packing}
Let $H=(V,E)$ be a finite hypergraph.  Suppose each $G\in\mathcal G$ has an
integer demand $r(G)\geq0$ such that every vertex cover $C$ satisfies
$|C\cap G|\geq r(G)$.  If nonnegative rational weights $y_G$ satisfy
\[
 \sum_{G\ni v}y_G\leq1\qquad(v\in V),
\]
then every vertex cover satisfies
\[
 |C|\geq\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}r(G)y_G.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Exact double counting gives
\[
 \sum_G r(G)y_G
 \leq\sum_G|C\cap G|y_G
 =\sum_{v\in C}\sum_{G\ni v}y_G
 \leq|C|.
\]
\end{proof}

In particular, an objective strictly greater than $k-1$ certifies integral
cover number at least $k$.  The committed JSON contains exact rational
packings at $271$ prefixes, producing $274$ certified cover levels.  The
verifier regenerates every configuration and checks every rational load and
strict objective inequality.  If $t_k$ is the first certified threshold for
level $k$, the resulting exact prefix strength is
\[
 S=\sum_{k=1}^{274}\frac1{t_k}
  =\frac{3251333}{4989600}.
\]

\section{Disjoint multiplier blocks and the upper constant}

Write $Q=\prod p^{e_p}$.  Choose multipliers whose $p$-adic valuations are
multiples of $e_p+1$; explicitly the moduli are $8,5,3,3,2$ at
$2,3,5,7,11$.  If $md=m'd'$ with $d,d'\mid Q$, reducing the valuations
modulo $e_p+1$ recovers every exponent of $d$ and $d'$.  Hence $d=d'$ and
then $m=m'$, so the blocks $mD$ are pairwise disjoint.

For rigor, first restrict each valuation multiple to a fixed finite range
$0\leq j<R$.  Elementary inclusion--exclusion and finite geometric sums give
a multiplier density $\rho_R$; after $N\to\infty$ one may let $R\to\infty$,
obtaining
\[
 \rho=\prod_{p^e\parallel Q}
       \frac{1-1/p}{1-p^{-(e+1)}}
     =\frac{23520}{110143}.
\]
This finite-cutoff order is essential; no interchange with an untruncated
infinite sum is used.

Let $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ be triple-free.  In every block, the omitted
vertices form a cover of the applicable divisor prefix.  Level $k$ therefore
forces one additional omission whenever $m\leq N/t_k$.  Disjointness permits
summing these losses.  First $N\to\infty$ at fixed $R$, and then
$R\to\infty$, gives
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{N-f(N)}N
 \geq \rho S
 =\frac{22759331}{163562355}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N
 \leq1-\rho S
 =\frac{140803024}{163562355},
\]
which completes Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}.

\section{Verification scope and disclosure}

The upper packing was discovered with AI-assisted search and a
floating-point LP solver.  The published claim depends only on the committed
exact rational certificate and the program's standard-library verification
path.  That path is run under normal and optimized Python, regenerates the
complete finite instance, pins its digest and target sequence, and includes
deliberate corruption tests.

The candidate lower route imports analytic input from pinned Della Pietra
Problem 301/327 Lean developments.  The repository contains source for the
structured wrapper, literal Problem 302 bridge, padding lemma, and cardinality
calculation, but exact-pins integration is still in progress.  Consequently
the lower conclusion is not a theorem claim of this draft.  The workflow
withholds the release PDF until the separate lower Lean build reproduces the
committed transitive axiom report; a root-project build alone does not
establish the lower statement.  At the pinned revisions, the external
repositories have no license file, so their source is not vendored here.

AI systems assisted with search, code generation, proof audits, and
formalization.  This assistance does not replace author or referee review.
Both the external work and this manuscript are unrefereed; a named independent
human review remains desirable before journal submission.
