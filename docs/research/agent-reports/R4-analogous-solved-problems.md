## 0. Executive summary

1. **Translation (OWN, elementary, proved in §1).** An odd head `a` in `(N/4, N/2)` has a 302-conflict with two even tails in `[N/2, N]` **iff `a` has two divisors `d < d'` with `d'/d ∈ (1, λ(θ)]`**, where `θ = a/N` and `λ(θ) = min(θ/(1/2−θ), (1−θ)/θ)` (`λ = 2` at `θ = 1/3`, `λ → 1` at `θ → 1/4, 1/2`). Equivalently `a` is divisible by `xy` for coprime `x < y ≤ λx`. Thus the "conflict problem" of 302 is *literally* the Erdős propinquity problem (Erdős Problem #144, Maier–Tenenbaum 1984) with a `θ`-dependent window, and the tail side is a *localized factorization* problem in the sense of Koukoulopoulos (b is a tail iff `b` is divisible by a product `x·(x+y)` of two coprime divisors in adjacent dyadic windows, `x ∈ (X,2X]`, `x+y ∈ (2X,4X]`).
2. **Consequence (proved by Maier–Tenenbaum, REF):** for every fixed `L`, the proportion of *L-rough* odd heads in `(N/4+εN, N/2−εN)` that are conflict-free against the full top half tends to 0. So no construction of the type "Cambie ∪ {conflict-free heads}" — with or without roughness — gives `5/8 + c` for an explicit `c > 0`; something must be deleted from the top half. The observed 0.73–0.80 densities at `N ≤ 3·10^6` are finite-size effects with decay exponents between `0.004` (Stef) and `0.0986` (Erdős–Hall/Maier–Tenenbaum) in `log N`.
3. **Why Della Pietra's argument nevertheless works (OWN reading of his §§3–5 in the light of Ford/Koukoulopoulos):** the density of integers carrying a conflict *at scale X* (divisor pair `x<y<2x` near `X`, or tail factorization `x(x+y)` near `X`) is `≍ (log X)^{-Q(1/log√3)}(log log X)^{-3/2} = (log X)^{-0.2702…}(…)` (Koukoulopoulos, REF, for `k = 2`); the exponent is `< 1`, so the sum over dyadic scales diverges and typical integers carry conflicts (Maier–Tenenbaum). The integers that carry a scale-`X` conflict are exactly those with about `1.82·log log X` prime factors below `X` (1.82 = 1/log√3) instead of the typical `log log X`. Della Pietra's "centered regularity" `Ω_L(n,x) ≤ A log(log x/log L)+K` removes precisely these atypical integers from both heads and tails; the Rankin trick then turns the per-scale exponent from `−0.27` into `E < −1`, so the dyadic sum converges and the total is `O(N(log L)^{-2+θ})` against a source `≍ N/log L`. This is the *same mechanism* as Ford's proof that `H(x,y,2y) = o(x)` and as Besicovitch's construction of primitive sets of upper density `→ 1/2`. It gives only qualitative constants because the gain is `≍ ρ_L·(fraction surviving)` with `L` huge.
4. **Closest solved analogue with the same "top interval + lower structure" shape: 3-term geometric-progression-free sets** (Rankin 1960; Riddell 1969; Nathanson–O'Bryant 2015; McNew, Math. Comp. 2015, REF). McNew computes the supremum of the upper density exactly via a *finite smooth-tile exclusion count* — the multiplicative twin of van Doorn's `9/10` argument — and obtains `0.730027 < α < 0.772059` (rational ratios) and `0.815509 < β < 0.819222` (integer ratios). His lower-bound constructions are (i) unions of intervals `(N/48,N/45] ∪ (N/40,N/36] ∪ (N/32,N/27] ∪ (N/24,N/12] ∪ (N/9,N/8] ∪ (N/4,N]` (density `3523/4320`), and (ii) "Rankin's exponent set restricted to the coprime-to-6 part, with an optimised finite 2,3-smooth tile". The **upper-bound machinery transfers verbatim** to 302 (it is the method of the repository's `0.8609`); the **lower-bound machinery does not transfer directly**, because a 302 triple is *tight* (`b/a < 2`, `c/a < 3`, `c/b < 2`) whereas a geometric progression is *spread* (`ar²/a ≥ 4`, resp. `≥ 9/4`), so unions of full intervals below `N/2` are never 302-free (§8). But McNew's *exact finite tile* idea suggests the finite "block-optimisation" that both the upper-bound side and a lower-bound side could share (§10, idea 3).
5. **Product-free sets (Kurlberg–Lagarias–Pomerance, Acta Arith. 2012, REF):** density arbitrarily close to 1 via the `Ω`-window trick `{m : z < Ω(m) < 2z}`. The trick does *not* transfer to 302: for a triple `(kxy, kx(x+y), ky(x+y))` the differences `Ω(b)−Ω(a) = Ω(x+y)−Ω(y)` and `Ω(c)−Ω(a) = Ω(x+y)−Ω(x)` are unconstrained, and the only "per-prime valuation" class closed under the relation is the odd numbers (§1.3, §4).
6. **Primitive sets:** Besicovitch's construction (`sup d̄ = 1/2`, 1934) is the ancestor of every "top block + sparse lower blocks" construction; the reason it works — the density of integers with a divisor in `(y,2y]` tends to 0 — is exactly Ford's theorem. For 302 the analogous statement is *false* in the relevant window (typical integers **do** have a divisor pair with ratio in `(1,λ]`), which is the whole difficulty (§2).
7. **Sets with no `a | bc` (Erdős 1938, REF):** every such set is automatically 302-free (`bc = a(b+c)`), but they have size only `π(N) + Θ(N^{2/3}/(log N)^2)`. Multiplicative Sidon sets (`ab ≠ cd`) are *not* related to 302 (§3).
8. **Where an explicit constant could come from (§10):** the only route consistent with all of the above is an *explicit* version of the Ford/Della Pietra mechanism — bound the number of top-half integers with an atypical prime-factor profile by an *explicit* Hardy–Ramanujan/Halberstam–Richert inequality, and bound the small-scale conflicts by an explicit finite sieve — plus a *finite* block optimisation à la McNew for the structure of the deleted tails. Realistic outcome: `5/8 + 10^{-4}`-type constants, not `0.66`; the numerics of §9 (gain `0.0296 N` at `N = 3·10^6` from `97`-rough heads, `0.10 N` from all odd heads) quantify the price of roughness.

## 1. Dictionary: the 302 conflict structure in divisor language (OWN, elementary)

Solutions with `a < b < c`: `a = kxy, b = kx(x+y), c = ky(x+y)`, `gcd(x,y) = 1`, `x < y`, `k ≥ 1` (shared context). Put `θ = a/N`.

### 1.1 Heads. 
Both tails lie in `[N/2, N]` iff `1 + x/y ≥ 1/(2θ)` and `1 + y/x ≤ 1/θ`, i.e.
```
y/x ∈ (1, λ(θ)],   λ(θ) := min( θ/(1/2−θ), (1−θ)/θ ).
```
`λ(1/4) = 1`, `λ(1/3) = 2` (maximum), `λ(1/2) = 1`. So a head `a ≈ θN` is in a conflict iff `a` is divisible by `xy` with `x<y≤λ(θ)x` coprime.

**Lemma (equivalence with close divisors).** `a` has coprime `x<y≤λx` with `xy | a` **iff** `a` has two divisors `d<d'≤λd`. Proof: `⇐`: let `g = gcd(d,d')`, `x = d/g`, `y = d'/g`; then `x,y` are coprime divisors of `a`, hence `xy | a`. `⇒`: take `d = x, d' = y`. For odd `a` all of `k,x,y` are odd, `x+y` is even, so both tails are even. ∎

Hence: **an odd head `a ∈ (N/4,N/2)` is conflict-free against the full top half iff `a` has no two divisors with ratio in `(1, λ(a/N)]`**. Korsky's "half-isolated" criterion (no `d | a²` in `[a/2,a)`) is the `λ = 2` case, which is uniform in `N`.

### 1.2 Tails. 
`b = kx(x+y)` is divisible by the coprime pair `x`, `x+y` with `(x+y)/x ∈ (2,3)`; `c = ky(x+y)` by the coprime pair `y`, `x+y` with `(x+y)/y ∈ (3/2, 2)`. Conversely, `c ∈ [N/2,N]` is a (large) tail of some triple with odd head in `(N/4,N/2)` iff `c` has divisors `D<D'` with `D'/D ∈ (1 + N/(2c), 2)`, with the parity conditions `y = D/g`, `x = (D'−D)/g` odd and `k = c g²/(DD')` odd (same `g`-argument). Counting the top-half integers divisible by `x(x+y)` with `x ∈ (X,2X]` is Koukoulopoulos's `H^{(3)}(N; (X,2X),(2X,4X))` (§5.3) up to constants.

### 1.3 Valuations. 
For each prime `p` the valuations of `(a,b,c)` are `(u+v_x, u+v_x, u)` if `p|x`, `(u+v_y, u, u+v_y)` if `p|y`, `(u, u+v, u+v)` if `p | x+y`, `(u,u,u)` otherwise (`u = v_p(k)`). So **in every triple, for every prime, the two equal valuations are the larger ones**; since `x, y, x+y` are pairwise coprime and `> 1`, *each* of `a, b, c` is the strictly-low element at some prime. For `p = 2`, one of `x, y, x+y` is even, so at most one element is odd (the closed class of odd numbers). For `p ≥ 3` no such pigeonhole exists (`x ≡ y ≡ 1 mod 3` gives `(28,44,77)`), so **no other "coprime to p" class is closed**, and no Rankin-type exponent set is closed either (the pattern `(u,u,u)` is always allowed).

## 2. (a) Primitive sets: Besicovitch, Behrend, Erdős, Martin–Pomerance, Lichtman

**Definitions/results (BOOK: Hall–Tenenbaum *Divisors*, Ch. 0; Halberstam–Roth *Sequences*, Ch. V; SITE #446; REF Lichtman–Pomerance Proc. AMS Ser. B 6 (2019) 1–14, arXiv:1806.02250; REF Lichtman, Forum Math. Pi 2023, arXiv:2202.02384; ARX Martin–Pomerance arXiv:1009.1014.)*

* Finite version: the largest primitive subset of `{1,…,n}` has exactly `⌊(n+1)/2⌋` elements (Nathanson–O'Bryant arXiv:1408.2880, Theorem 1, restating a classical fact) — the top half `(n/2, n]` is extremal and the odd numbers are *not* primitive. This is the "`k = 2` geometric progression" problem.
* Infinite version — **Besicovitch (1934):** `sup_{A primitive} d̄(A) = 1/2`, disproving Davenport's conjecture. Construction: `N_1 ≪ N_2 ≪ …`, `A = ⋃_i {n ∈ (N_i, 2N_i] : n has no divisor in ⋃_{j<i}(N_j,2N_j]}`. It works because `δ(y) := d{n : n has a divisor in (y,2y]}` satisfies `liminf δ(y) = 0` (Besicovitch) and in fact `δ(y) → 0` (Erdős 1935); `δ(y) = (log y)^{-δ+o(1)}` (Erdős 1960) with `δ = 1 − (1+log log 2)/log 2 = 0.08607…`; `δ(y) ≍ (log y)^{-δ}(log log y)^{-3/2}` (Ford 2008). [SITE #446 lists exactly this chain.]
* **Behrend (1935), Erdős (1935):** every primitive `A` has lower density 0; Behrend: `Σ_{a∈A, a≤x} 1/a ≪ log x/√(log log x)`; **Erdős (1935):** `Σ_{a∈A} 1/(a log a) ≤ C` uniformly (REF; Erdős's conjecture that the primes maximise this sum was proved by Lichtman 2022/23).
* **Martin–Pomerance (arXiv:1009.1014, REF Acta Arith. 2011):** for any `F` with `∫ F(t)/(t² log t) dt < ∞` (and mild regularity) there is a primitive `S` with `S(x) ≍ F(x)`; i.e. primitive sets can have counting function as large as `x/((log x)(log log x)^{1+ε})`-type, but never positive lower density.

**Transfer to 302.** Cambie's set `{odd ≤ N/4} ∪ [N/2,N]` is a two-block Besicovitch-type set; the infinite analogue `⋃_i ({odd ≤ N_i/4} ∪ [N_i/2, N_i])` with `N_{i+1} > 12 N_i` has upper density `5/8` (OWN, one-line check with §1.3: a triple with head in a lower block would need two even elements below `3N_i`, which the next block does not supply). The Besicovitch principle "add a block of heads and delete the few top-half elements they hit" needs `#{tails hit} = o(#heads)`; by §1.1–1.2 and Maier–Tenenbaum this **fails** for any positive-density block of heads and any fixed roughness — the analogue of `δ(y) → 0` is false in the window `(1,λ]` — unless the heads and tails are also restricted by a Ford-type prime-factor profile (§6.4). So the primitive-set template gives the *shape* of the construction but the *analytic input must be the opposite one*: not "a divisor in a window is rare" but "a divisor pair in a window is carried only by integers with an atypical profile".

## 3. (b) Multiplicative Sidon sets, `ab = cd`, and `a ∤ bc`

* **Erdős (1938, Tomsk; 1969), REF:** the maximum size of a multiplicative Sidon set (`ab = cd ⇒ {a,b} = {c,d}`) in `{1,…,n}` is `π(n) + Θ(n^{3/4}/(log n)^{3/2})` (lower order term settled 1969). Counting version: Liu–Pach, J. Combin. Theory A (2019), arXiv:1808.06182: the number of multiplicative Sidon subsets of `[n]` is `T(n)·2^{Θ(n^{3/4}/(log n)^{3/2})}`. Multiplicative 3-Sidon: Pach, arXiv:1801.08733, `≤ π(n)+π(n/2)+n^{2/3}(log n)^{2^{1/3}−1/3+o(1)}`.
* **Erdős (1938), REF:** the maximum size of `A ⊂ [n]` such that **no element divides the product of two others** is `π(n) + Θ(n^{2/3}/(log n)^2)`; Chan–Győri–Sárközy (2010) for `k` others: `π(n) + Θ_k(n^{2/(k+1)}/(log n)^2)`.

**Transfer to 302 (OWN).** `1/a = 1/b+1/c ⇔ bc = a(b+c)`, so `a | bc`: **every "no `a | bc`" set is 302-free**, but such sets have size `~ π(n)`, i.e. density 0 — useless for a linear bound, though it shows that the 302 relation is a *divisibility* relation `a | bc` sharpened by `(b+c) | bc`. Multiplicative Sidon sets are unrelated (a 302 triple does not produce an `ab = cd` coincidence inside `A`). The Erdős `n^{2/3}` technique (graph of products of "small" primes, bipartite Turán counting) is the model for the *upper-bound* side of 301/302 (disjoint tiles), not for lower bounds.

## 4. (c) Product-free sets (`ab ≠ c`)

**Kurlberg–Lagarias–Pomerance, "Product-free sets with high density", Acta Arith. 155 (2012) 163–173, arXiv:1107.5589 (REF).**
* Theorem 2.1: there are infinitely many `n` with `D(n) > 1 − C/(log log n)^{1 − (e log 2)/2}`, exponent `1 − (e log 2)/2 ≈ 0.057915`, where `D(n)` is the maximal density of a product-free subset of `Z/nZ`; Corollary 2.2: product-free sets of integers of density `> 1−ε` for every `ε`. (A sequel makes the rate `(log log n)^{-0.0579…}(log log log n)^{-1/2}` and shows it is sharp.)
* Remark 2.7: a product-free set with least element `a` has upper density `≤ 1 − 1/(2a)`.
* Mechanism: `{m : z < Ω(m) < 2z}` is product-free; with `z ≈ (e/4) log log x` this captures almost all `m ≤ x` (Hardy–Ramanujan concentration); Lemma 2.3 makes it periodic: for `D` a product-free set of divisors of `n/rad(n)`, `S_D = {s mod n : gcd(s,n) ∈ D}` is product-free with `|S_D| = φ(n) Σ_{d∈D} 1/d`.
* Pomerance–Schinzel (2011): `D(n) < 1/2` unless `n` is divisible by a square with ≥ 6 distinct prime factors. In `[1,N]` the trivial product-free set `(N/2,N]` already has density `1/2`.

**Transfer to 302 (OWN).** The `Ω`-window trick relies on `Ω(ab) = Ω(a)+Ω(b)`; for 302, `Ω(b)−Ω(a) = Ω(x+y)−Ω(y)`, `Ω(c)−Ω(a) = Ω(x+y)−Ω(x)` can take any values, so no `Ω`-window or `Ω`-congruence class is 302-free (`Ω(x+y)` is "random"). The `gcd(s,n) ∈ D` periodisation is likewise blocked by §1.3. What does transfer is the *philosophy* "restrict to integers with a prescribed prime-factor statistic at every scale" — which is exactly Della Pietra's centered regularity — and the observation that such restrictions cost only a `(log log)^{-c}` factor when centred at the mean, but a *power of `log`* when centred away from the mean (this is why regular sets with `A > 1` have positive density while `A < 1` would not; §6.4).

## 5. (d) Ford's `H(x,y,z)`, the multiplication table, rough integers, localized factorizations

### 5.1 Ford, "The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval", Ann. of Math. 168 (2008) 367–433, arXiv:math/0401223 (REF).
Notation (Ford (1.2)–(1.3)): `z = e^η y = y^{1+u}`, `η = (log y)^{-β}`, `β = log 4 − 1 + ξ/√(log log y)`, `z_0(y) = y·exp{(log y)^{1−log 4}} ≈ y + y/(log y)^{log 4 −1}`, `G(β) = ((1+β)/log 2)·log((1+β)/(e log 2)) + 1` for `0 ≤ β ≤ log 4 −1`, `G(β) = β` for `β ≥ log 4 − 1`; `δ = G(0) = 1 − (1+log log 2)/log 2 = 0.086071…`.
**Theorem 1 (v):** for `x > 10^5`, `100 ≤ y ≤ z−1`, `y ≤ √x`,
```
H(x,y,z)/x ≍  η                                         (y+1 ≤ z ≤ z_0(y))
           ≍  β / ( max(1,−ξ) (log y)^{G(β)} )            (z_0(y) ≤ z ≤ 2y)
           ≍  u^δ (log(2/u))^{-3/2}                       (2y ≤ z ≤ y²)
           ≍  1                                           (z ≥ y²).
```
**Corollary 1:** `H(x_1,y_1,z_1)/x_1 ≍ H(x_2,y_2,z_2)/x_2` whenever `log(z_i/y_i)`, `log y_i`, `log(x_i/z_i)` are pairwise comparable — in particular, for fixed `λ ∈ (1,2]`, `H(x,y,λy) ≍_λ x/((log y)^δ (log log y)^{3/2})`. **Corollary 3 (multiplication table):** `A(x) = #{n ≤ x : n = m_1 m_2, m_i ≤ √x} ≍ x/((log x)^δ (log log x)^{3/2})`. **Theorem 4:** `H_1(x,y,z)/H(x,y,z) ≍ log log(z/y+10)/log(z/y+10)` for `y+1 ≤ z ≤ x^{5/8}`, `yz ≤ x^{1−c}` (so for `z = 2y` a positive proportion of the integers with a divisor in `(y,2y]` have exactly one — disproving Erdős's `δ_1 = o(δ)` guess, SITE #446). **Theorem 5:** for `r ≥ 2`, `z_0(y) ≤ z ≤ 10y`: `max(1,−ξ)/√(log log y) ≪_r H_r/H ≤ 1`. **Theorem 6/7 (shifted primes):** `H(x,y,z;P_λ) ≪ H(x,y,z)/log x`. Heuristic (Ford §1.5): only the primes of `n` in `(z/y, z]` matter, and the integers counted have `k − k_0 = O(1)` prime factors there with `k_0 = ⌊(log log z − log η)/log 2⌋` — i.e. `1/log 2 = 1.44` times the typical number.

### 5.2 Ford, "Integers with a divisor in (y,2y]", arXiv:math/0607473 (REF, Anatomy of Integers 2008): short proof of `H(x,y,2y) ≍ x/((log y)^δ(log log y)^{3/2})`, `3 ≤ y ≤ √x`.

### 5.3 Koukoulopoulos, "Localized factorizations of integers", Proc. LMS 101 (2010) 392–426, arXiv:0809.1072 (REF).
`H^{(k+1)}(x, y, 2y) := #{n ≤ x : n divisible by d_1⋯d_k with y_i < d_i ≤ 2y_i}`. With `Q(u) = u log u − u + 1` and `ρ = (k+1)^{1/k}`:
**Theorem 1:** for `k ≥ 2`, `0 < δ ≤ 1`, `3 ≤ y_1 ≤ … ≤ y_k`, `2^{k+1} y_1⋯y_k ≤ x/y_1^δ`:
`H^{(k+1)}(x,y,2y) ≪_{k,δ} (log y_k/log y_1)^{k+1} · x/((log y_1)^{Q(1/log ρ)}(log log y_1)^{3/2})`, and `≫_{k,δ,c}` the same without the first factor if `y_k ≤ y_1^c`. Corollary: `A_{k+1}(N) ≍ N^{k+1}/((log N)^{Q(1/log ρ)}(log log N)^{3/2})` for the `(k+1)`-dimensional multiplication table. **Numerically (OWN):** `k=1`: `Q(1/log 2) = 0.086071`; **`k=2`: `ρ = √3`, `1/log ρ = 1.8205`, `Q = 0.27017`**; `k=3`: `0.50655`; `k=4`: `0.77734`.

### 5.4 Ford, "Rough integers with a divisor in a given interval", J. Aust. Math. Soc. (2019/20), arXiv:1901.02548 (REF).
`R_w` = integers with no prime factor `≤ w`. **Theorem 1:** for `4 ≤ y ≤ √x`, `4 ≤ w ≤ y/8`, `δ := log log w/log log y`: (i) if `1 − 1/log 4 ≤ δ ≤ 1`: `H(x,y,2y;R_w) ≍ x/log log w`; (ii) if `0 ≤ δ < 1 − 1/log 4`: `H(x,y,2y;R_w) ≍ x δ B(w,y) (log y)^{-E + log(1−δ)/log 2}`, `B = min(1, (log log y)^{-1/2}((1−δ)log 4 − 1)^{-1})`, `E = δ_{Ford}`. Remark 1: for `log log w ≤ √(log log y)` this is `≍ x·log log w/((log y)^E (log w)^{1/log 2}(log log y)^{3/2})`. (Duality remark: for `y > √x` use `d | n ⇔ (n/d) | n`.)

### 5.5 What these say about 302 (OWN).
* **Per-scale conflict density.** By §1.1–1.2, "head `a` has a conflict at scale `X`" and "`b` is a tail at scale `X`" are `H^{(3)}`-events (two coprime divisors in adjacent dyadic windows whose product divides `n`). Koukoulopoulos gives their density `≍ (log X)^{-0.2702}(log log X)^{-3/2}` (the coprimality/parity/ratio-window conditions change constants only). **The exponent `0.27 < 1` is the quantitative reason the dyadic sum over scales diverges** — heuristically `Σ_{X=2^j ≤ √N} j^{-0.27}(log j)^{-3/2} ≍ (log N)^{0.73}/(log log N)^{3/2}` scales per integer — so almost every head (rigorously: Maier–Tenenbaum) and, heuristically, almost every top-half even number is eventually involved. The integers carrying a scale-`X` conflict have `≈ 1.82 log log X` prime factors below `X`.
* **Roughness alone does not help asymptotically** (Ford 5.4 quantifies this for one window: `H(x,y,2y;R_w)` loses only a factor `(log w)^{1/log 2}/log log w`, not a power of `log y`), which matches the numerics of §9 (for fixed `L` the conflict-free share of `L`-rough heads still drifts down).
* **A Ford-type theorem for divisors of `a²`** is not needed: by the Lemma of §1.1 the relevant event on `a²` is equivalent to a two-divisor event on `a` itself.

## 6. (e) Hall–Tenenbaum *Divisors*, Hooley's `Δ`, propinquity, regular prime-factor distribution, de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum

### 6.1 Propinquity (Erdős Problem #144, SITE, and REF sources).
* **Erdős–Hall, "The propinquity of divisors", Bull. LMS 11 (1979):** the set of `n` having divisors `d_1<d_2<d_1(1+(log n)^{-β})` has density 0 if `β > log 3 − 1 = 0.09861…`.
* **Maier–Tenenbaum, Invent. Math. 76 (1984) 121–128 (REF):** the density of integers with two divisors `d_1<d_2<2d_1` is 1 (Erdős's 1948 conjecture, $650 prize; also for `2` replaced by any `c>1`); their proof gives density 1 for `β < log 3 − 1`. Hence `E_1(n) := min_{d<d'|n} log(d'/d) = (log n)^{-(log 3 −1)+o(1)}` for almost all `n`.
* **Raouj–Stef–Tenenbaum, Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 150 (2011) 73–96 (REF), quoted in Tenenbaum's survey arXiv:1908.00488 (§2):** `E_1(n) = (log n)/(3^{ω(n)}(log_2 n)^{ϑ_n})` p.p. with `−5 ≤ ϑ_n ≤ 10`; `E_r(n) ≤ (log n)^{-β_r+o(1)}` p.p., `β_r = (log 3 −1)^m/(log 3 − 1/3)^{m−1}` for `2^{m−1} < r+1 ≤ 2^m` (`β_1 = 0.09861`, `β_2=β_3 ≈ 0.01271`, `β_r ≈ 0.00164` for `4 ≤ r ≤ 7`); `E_r(n) > τ(n)^{-1/r+o(1)}` p.p. uniformly in `r`.
* **Stef, thèse Nancy 1992 (unpublished thesis; quoted in arXiv:1908.00488 eq. (8)):** the exceptional set `R_x = #{n ≤ x : no d<d'≤2d}` satisfies `x/(log x)^{β+o(1)} ≪ R_x ≪ x e^{-c√(log log x)}`, `β = 1 − (1+log log 3)/log 3 = 0.0041547…` (upper bound Maier–Tenenbaum). "Best known estimates to date" (Tenenbaum 2019). These are the integers Korsky calls half-isolated; they form the only *`N`-uniform* conflict-free class of heads, and it has density 0.
* **Erdős Problems #448/#449 (SITE, disproved):** `τ^+(n) < ε τ(n)` and `r(n) < ε τ(n)` (`r(n)` = number of divisor pairs `d_1<d_2<2d_1`) both fail on a positive-density set (Erdős–Tenenbaum 1981; Hall–Tenenbaum *Divisors* §4.6); Ford: `Σ_{n≤x} τ^+(n) ≍ x(log x)^{1−δ}/(log log x)^{3/2}`. **Consequence for 302 (OWN):** a positive proportion of heads have `≫ τ(a)` conflicts, i.e. the conflict hypergraph has hubs on the head side as well — relevant for hitting-set arguments.

### 6.2 Hooley's `Δ` function (BOOK Hall–Tenenbaum *Divisors*, Cambridge Tracts 90, 1988, Ch. 4–7; REF as listed).
`Δ(n) = max_u #{d | n : e^u < d ≤ e^{u+1}}`. Hall–Tenenbaum 1982: `Σ_{n≤x} Δ(n) ≫ x log log x`; Hooley 1979: `≪ x (log x)^{4/π −1}`; Hall–Tenenbaum: `≪ x exp(c√(log log x))`. **Koukoulopoulos–Tao, "An upper bound on the mean value of the Erdős–Hooley Delta function" (2023):** `≪ x (log log x)^{11/4}`; **Ford–Koukoulopoulos–Tao, arXiv:2308.11987 (Proc. LMS, accepted):** `Σ_{n≤x} Δ(n) ≫_ε x (log log x)^{1+η−ε}`, `η = 0.3533227…`; **Ford–Green–Koukoulopoulos, Invent. Math. 232 (2023), arXiv:1908.00378:** `Δ(n) ≥ (log log n)^{0.35332277…}` for almost all `n` (disproving a Maier–Tenenbaum conjecture). **de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum, "Two upper bounds for the Erdős–Hooley Delta-function", arXiv:2210.13897:** improved average/normal-order upper bounds.
**Relevance:** `Δ(a) ≥ 2` in base `λ` is exactly "a has a conflict" (§1.1); the *normal order* `(log log n)^{0.35}` says typical heads have *many* conflicts, not just one; and the mean value `x(log log x)^{1+η}` is the right normalisation for "conflicts per head" if one insists on the whole class of odd heads (the total conflict count `0.0043 N log N` is dominated by hubs, not by typical heads).

### 6.3 Regular prime-factor distribution.
* **Tenenbaum, *Introduction to analytic and probabilistic number theory*, Thm III.3.5 (BOOK, Halberstam–Richert/Shiu-type):** for non-negative multiplicative `g` with `Σ_{p≤y} g(p) log p ≪ y` and a prime-power condition, `Σ_{n≤x} g(n) ≪ (x/log x)·Σ_{n≤x} g(n)/n`. This is Della Pietra's Proposition "one-variable estimate" and Sawin's Lemma 4; combined with Rankin's trick it bounds `#{n ≤ Y : Ω_L(n,x) > A log(log x/log L)+K for some x}` by `≪ Y ρ_L z^{-K}` for `z − 1 < A log z` (which forces `A > 1`; Della Pietra Lemma "irregular", `main.tex` ll. 172–217).
* **de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum, "Mean values of arithmetic functions and application to sums of powers", Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 180 (2025) 1–13, arXiv:2403.19320 (REF).** Theorem 3.1: for primitive polynomials `Q_1,…,Q_k ∈ Z[X_1,…,X_t]` and `F ∈ M_k(A,B,ε)` (i.e. `F(ab) ≤ min{A^{Ω(℘a)}, B(℘a)^ε}F(b)` for coprime `a,b`), uniformly for boxes `x_j − y_j < n_j ≤ x_j` with `x_j^α ≤ y_j ≤ x_j` and `min x_j ≥ c(max x_j+‖Q‖)^β`, `Σ F(|Q_1(n)|,…,|Q_k(n)|) ≪ ℘y · E_R(s_x) Π_{p ≤ …}(…)` — a `t`-variable Nair–Tenenbaum/Henriot bound (`t = 1` is Henriot; binary forms are de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum Mathematika 2012). With `t = 2`, `k = 3`, `Q = (X_1, X_2, X_1+X_2)` this is the "three-form block" used by Sawin (Lemma 6) and Della Pietra (Proposition three-form, `main.tex` l. 320–360): `Σ_{X ≤ r < 2X, r<s<2r, (r,s)=1} g_1(r)g_2(s)g_3(r+s) ≪ X² (log X)^{(exponents)}`. (Theorem 1.1 of the paper is about `V_0(x)`, integers that are sums of powers, and is not used.)

### 6.4 The mechanism, made explicit (OWN synthesis; consistent with Della Pietra `main.tex` ll. 51–59, 320–360, 409–445, 528–563).
For an `L`-rough head `a` and a conflict `(x,y)` at scale `X` (i.e. `x,y ≍ X`, `x<y≤λx`): the tails `b = (kx)(x+y)` and `c = (ky)(x+y)` are products of two "generic" numbers each carrying a full complement of primes below `X`; so `Ω_L(b, X) ≈ Ω_L(a,X) − Ω_L(y,X) + Ω_L(x+y,X) ≈ 2 log(log X/log L)` for generic `a, x, y`, against a regularity cap `A_t log(log X/log L) + K_t` with `A_t < 2`. Hence **every conflict at scale `X > L^{exp(K_t/(2−A_t))}` has an irregular tail**, and the top-half set `T_N` (regular integers) automatically excludes it; Maier–Tenenbaum is not contradicted because the deleted irregular set has positive (small, `≍ z^{-K_t}`, `o_L(ρ_L)`) density and contains the hubs. At scales `L ≤ X ≤ L^{(log L)^{c'}}` the expected number of conflicts per `L`-rough head is `≍ ρ_L² · (λ−1) · #{dyadic scales} ≍ (log L)^{-2}·(log L)^{1+c'}`, which is `o(1)` for `c' < 1`; the Rankin weights implement this bookkeeping uniformly in `N` with exponent `E = M + 2γ + α + β − 4 < −1` (Della Pietra (eq. ME)). **Everything is explicit in principle** — Mertens with explicit constants, explicit Hardy–Ramanujan-type inequalities `#{n ≤ x : Ω(n,y) ≥ k} ≤ C x (log log y + B)^{k−1}/((k−1)! log y)`-type, explicit sieve upper bounds — but the gain is `≍ ρ_L·(1/8)·(1 − o(1))N` with `ρ_L ≈ 0.56/log L` and `L` must be large enough that `(log L)^{-1+c'}` beats the implied constants: realistic explicit constants are `10^{-3}`–`10^{-6}` at best (§9 shows that even the *numerical* gain from `97`-rough heads at `N = 3·10^6` is only `0.0296 N`).

## 7. (f) Sets with no two elements/divisors in a factor-2 window

* A *set* `A ⊂ [1,N]` with no two elements in ratio `(1,2)` is lacunary, `|A| ≤ log_2 N + 1` — trivial; the meaningful version is the *divisor* one: integers all of whose divisor ratios avoid `(1,2)` = Hooley `Δ(n) = 1` in base 2 = Stef's exceptional set (§6.1), of size `x/(log x)^{0.0041…}` to `x e^{-c√(log log x)}`. **These are exactly Korsky's half-isolated numbers; their `1/n`-sum diverges like `(log X)^{1−β}`, which is what gives his sub-linear `N/(log N)^{β+o(1)}` gain** (his `E_N = {mp : m half-isolated ≤ N^{1/2}/10, p prime in (N/4m, N/2m)}` is a Besicovitch-style "one large prime" lift).
* Erdős Problem #693 (SITE, open): gaps between integers in `[n,n^k]` having a divisor in `(n,2n)` — a "local" version of `H`; #450 (open): short-interval version. Neither helps directly.
* **Tenenbaum, "Sur la probabilité qu'un entier possède un diviseur dans un intervalle donné", Compositio 51 (1984) 243–263 (REF):** first sharp bounds for `H(x,y,z)` in all ranges (Ford's Theorem T1): (i) `H ∼ ηx` when `ξ → ∞`; (ii) `x/((log y)^{G(β)} Z(log y)) ≪ H ≪ x/((log y)^{G(β)} max(1,−ξ))` for `z ≤ 2y`; (iii) `x u^δ/Z(1/u) ≪ H ≪ x u^δ log log(3/u)/√(log(2/u))` for `2y ≤ z ≤ y^{3/2}`; (iv) `H = x(1+O(log y/log z))`.
* **Hall–Tenenbaum 1991** (Ford's account): `H(x,y,z) ≍ x/((log y)^{G(β)} max(1,−ξ))` near the threshold `z_0(y)`, with `H_1 ≍ H` there.

**Bound on the number of conflicts (OWN, first-moment, elementary; explains the shared-context heuristic `0.0043 N log N`).** `#conflicts = Σ_{x<y≤2x odd coprime} #{k odd : kxy ∈ (N/4,N/2), tails in range} ≈ (N/8)·Σ_x (1/x)·Σ_{y∈(x,λ̄x]} 1/y · (window factor) ≍ N log N`, because the inner sum is `≍ 1` per dyadic `x`-scale and there are `≍ log N` scales up to `√N`. Restricting to `L`-rough `x, y` multiplies by `ρ_L²` but does not remove the `log N`; only a scale cap (via profile regularity of tails) does.

## 8. The closest solved density analogue: 3-term geometric-progression-free sets

**Sources.** Rankin 1960 (REF); Riddell 1969 (`α ≤ 6/7`); Brown–Gordon (`< 0.86889`); Beiglböck–Bergelson–Hindman–Strauss; Nathanson–O'Bryant, "A problem of Rankin on sets without geometric progressions", Acta Arith. 170 (2015), arXiv:1408.2880 (REF): `α < 0.84948`; **McNew, "On sets of integers which contain no three terms in geometric progression", Math. Comp. 84 (2015) 2893–2910, arXiv:1310.2277 (REF).** Definitions: `α = sup d̄(A)` over rational-ratio-GP-free `A`, `β` the same for integer ratios.

**Exact statements (McNew).**
* Theorem 1.4 (Rankin): `G_3^* = {n : v_p(n) ∈ A_3^* ∀p}`, `A_3^* = {0,1,3,4,9,…}` (no digit 2 in base 3), is GP-free with density `(1/ζ(2)) Π_{i>0} ζ(3^i)/ζ(2·3^i) > 0.71974`. (Works because `(v_p(a), v_p(ar), v_p(ar²))` is a 3-AP — the multiplicative twin of §1.3, where 302 has *no* such closed exponent pattern.)
* Theorem 3.1: `S_N = (N/48,N/45] ∪ (N/40,N/36] ∪ (N/32,N/27] ∪ (N/24,N/12] ∪ (N/9,N/8] ∪ (N/4,N]` is integer-ratio-GP-free with `3523N/4320 > 0.815509 N` elements; stacking `S_{N_i}` with `N_i = 48² N_{i−1}²/N_1` gives `β > 0.815509`. (Basis: `(N/4,N]` is free since `nr² > N` for `r ≥ 2`; `(N/9,N/8]` is addable because `2n` falls in the gap `(N/8,N/4]`.)
* Theorem 3.2: `β > 0.72195` for *asymptotic density* via re-optimising the `2,3,5`-exponent patterns.
* **Theorem 4.1/4.2 (the exact method):** for `s`-smooth ratios, `α_s = 1 − (Π_{p≤s}(p−1)/p)·Σ_{j∈I_s} 1/n_j`, where `n_1<n_2<…` are the `s`-smooth numbers and `I_s` the indices at which the largest GP-free subset of `{n_1,…,n_j}` needs one more exclusion (for `s=3`: `4, 9, 16, 18, 32, 36, 64, 81, 96, 128, 144, 192, 243, 256, 288, 384, 486, 512, 576, 729, 864, 972, 1024, 1296, 1458, 1728, 1944, 2048, 2304, 2592, 3072, 3888, 4096, 4374, 5184, 5832`); `0.790470 < α_3 < 0.791266`; and `α_s` is *attained* as an upper density (the construction is "for each `b` coprime to the smooth primes take the optimal tile `b·(S_{n_j}` minus exclusions`)`"). Theorem 4.3: `α < 0.772059` (primes `2,3,5,7`); Theorem 4.4: `0.815509 < β < 0.819222`; Theorem 4.5: `0.730027 < α` (2,3-optimised tile × Rankin on the coprime-to-6 part). Theorem 2.3: in `Z/nZ` one cannot keep a positive proportion (`E(Z/nZ) ≪ n (log log n)^5/(log n)^{1/2}`).

**Transfer to 302 (OWN).**
1. *Upper bounds:* McNew's `Σ_{j∈I_s} 1/n_j`-formula is exactly the disjoint-smooth-tile method of van Doorn (9/10), Wang, Della Pietra (5040) and the repository (`Q = 139 708 800`, `0.8609`) — and his Theorem 4.2 says the method is **sharp for smooth-ratio-restricted problems**. The analogous statement for 302 would be: `lim f_S(N)/N` exists for "conflicts whose parameters `k,x,y,x+y` are `S`-smooth" and equals `1 − (Π_{p∈S}(1−1/p))·Σ (exclusion increments)/n_j`. This gives a *conceptual ceiling* for the tile method and a way to *certify optimality* of finite tiles, but no lower bound for the true problem.
2. *Lower bounds:* the interval-union constructions fail for 302 because a 302 triple is tight: `1 < b/a < 2 < c/a < 3`, `1 < c/b < 2` (from `y/x ∈ (1,2)` in the window). Any interval `(θ_1 N, θ_2 N)` of heads below `N/2` with all integers included contains, for almost all its heads, a conflict with tails in `[N/2,N]` (§1.1 + Maier–Tenenbaum); the same holds for heads below `N/4` with tails in lower intervals. So the archimedean part of a 302 construction is forced to be `[N/2,N]` (plus a density-zero set), and all further gain must come from the *parity/valuation structure* (odd numbers) — which is why Cambie's `5/8` mixes an interval with a residue class, unlike anything in the GP literature.
3. *What is genuinely new for 302 in McNew's Theorem 4.5:* "optimise a finite tile in the small primes, then fill the coprime part with the best known infinite structure" — for 302 this suggests optimising, exactly (MILP), the best solution-free subset of `{b·t : t | Q}` for a `2,3,5,7`-smooth `Q` **on the union of the top half and the odd lower part**, and then proving the tile-sum formula. The exact optima at `N = 1000–4000` (top half minus 2 % highly composite evens; 95 % of odds; 44 % of evens below `N/2`) look like such a tile structure.

## 10. Ideas for the 302 lower bound suggested by the analogues (ranked; all OWN unless a source is named)

1. **Make the Ford/Della Pietra mechanism explicit (only route to an explicit constant that is consistent with §1–§6).** Ingredients all exist in explicit form: explicit Mertens (Rosser–Schoenfeld), explicit Hardy–Ramanujan/Halberstam–Richert bounds for `#{n ≤ x : Ω(n,y) ≥ k}`, explicit Brun–Titchmarsh/Selberg upper sieve for the three-form count at *bounded* scales (finitely many `(x,y)` pairs up to `X_0 = L^{C}` can even be enumerated). Plan: heads = odd `L`-rough `a ∈ (N/4,N/2)` with no conflict at scale `< X_0` (a finite, computable sieve condition of explicit density); tails removed `R` = top-half integers with `Ω(b, X_0) ≥ A log log X_0 + K` (explicit density), plus the tails of the finitely many small-scale patterns; prove that any conflict at scale `≥ X_0` has a tail in `R` using the valuation bookkeeping of §6.4 — this last step is where Della Pietra needs de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum, but with `X_0` fixed and `N → ∞` a cruder explicit Shiu-type bound may suffice. Why it might work: every step is a standard explicit sieve estimate. Main risk: the resulting `ε` is astronomically small (the gain is `≍ (1/8)·ρ_L·(1−c/log L)^{…}`, and `ρ_{97} ≈ 0.12` already costs a factor 8), so it does not approach `0.66`; also the tail deletion must be `o(ρ_L N)`, which forces `K` large and `X_0` huge.
2. **Prove a `θ`-resolved finite-`N` theorem instead of an asymptotic constant.** Near `θ = 1/4` and `1/2` the window `λ(θ) − 1 → 0` and the first-moment count of conflicts is `≍ (λ−1)·N·log N` — for heads in `(N/4, N/4 + ηN)` only divisor pairs with `x ≳ 1/(λ−1)` matter, and their number is *bounded* by `N/(4x_0)`-type sums. One gets rigorous statements of the form `f(N) ≥ (5/8 + g(N))N` with `g(N) ≍ 1/log N`-ish explicit — sub-linear but with far better constants than Korsky's `(log N)^{-0.004}` route for all practical `N`. Risk: still `o(N)`.
3. **McNew-style exact tile optimisation on the mixed structure (top half ∪ odds ∪ some evens).** Fix a `2,3,5,7`-smooth `Q` and, for `b` coprime to `210`, find by MILP the largest 302-free subset of `{bt : t | Q, bt ≤ N}` *subject to the global structure*, then prove the tile-sum formula à la McNew Theorem 4.2 for the "`210`-smooth-parameter" sub-problem. Why: the exact optima at `N ≤ 4000` show precisely this kind of tile structure (delete `~2 %` highly composite top-half elements, keep `44 %` of low evens), and McNew shows the smooth-restricted problem has an exactly computable limit that is *attained*. Risk: the smooth-restricted limit is only an upper bound for the true problem (conflicts with large `x, y` are ignored); for a lower bound one must additionally control non-smooth conflicts, which brings back idea 1.
4. **Hitting-set formulation with hub deletion (from #448/#449 and the tail table).** The conflict hypergraph has `≍ N log N` edges but the degrees are extremely skewed (Erdős–Tenenbaum/Hall–Tenenbaum: `r(n) ≫ τ(n)` on a positive-density set; Ford–Green–Koukoulopoulos: `Δ(n) ≥ (log log n)^{0.35}` typically). Delete the `≍ N/(log N)^{c}` hubs (integers with `Ω(n) ≥ (1+ε)log log N` or with a divisor in a fixed short window) from both sides and bound the remaining edges by a first moment — this is the "combinatorial" reading of idea 1 and may be provable with Ford's `H_r` theorems (which count integers with exactly `r` divisors in a window). Risk: after removing hubs the remaining edge count is still `≍ N·(log N)^{1 − Q}`-type unless the profile restriction is applied at *every* scale, i.e. exactly Della Pietra's regularity.
5. **Sawin's orientation trick as a computable one-sided rule (arXiv:2607.15419, Lemma 3).** Keep `a` iff it is the `Ω`-maximal element of every conflict containing it (or a weighted variant using `Ω(x+y)` vs `Ω(y)`); by §1.3 each element of a triple is "low" at some prime, so an orientation by valuation vectors is natural. Risk: Sawin's own remark that this gives constants far from optimal and that the odd-number bound is not beaten for the `a+b ∤ ab` question.
6. **Abandon the "conflict-free head" paradigm entirely and use the even numbers below `N/2` (the MILP signal).** Layered sets `⋃_j 2^j A_j` reduce to a multi-scale version of the same counting problem (cross-triples have valuation pattern `(v; v+j, v+j)`); the analogues (Rankin's exponent sets, KLP's `gcd(s,n)`-classes) show that *periodic/valuation* structures can carry density far above the interval structures, but §1.3 shows 302 has no closed exponent class beyond parity. Risk: no analogue provides a template; this is genuinely new territory.

## 11. Open questions

* Is the *set of top-half evens that are tails* of density `→ 1` (as the `(log N)^{0.73}` heuristic of §5.5 and the slow growth `0.177 → 0.209` suggest), or does it converge to a constant `< 1`? A Behrend-sequence argument (Hall–Tenenbaum, Ch. 1) on the sequence `{x(x+y) : x<y<2x coprime}` should decide it; if the density is 1, "top half minus a small set ∪ all odds" is dead and only structured (profile-restricted) sets can work.
* Minimum vertex cover of the conflict hypergraph on `(odd heads in (N/4,N/2)) × (even tails in [N/2,N])`: is it `(c+o(1))N` with `c > 0`, and what is `c`? Idea 4 gives the framework; the answer bounds the best "Cambie-type" set.
* Is there a Koukoulopoulos-type theorem for `H^{(3)}` restricted to `w`-rough integers (analogue of Ford 2019 for `k = 2`)? It would give the *exact* per-scale conflict density for rough heads and quantify how large `L` must be in Della Pietra's argument.
* Can McNew's exactness theorem (Theorem 4.2) be transplanted to 302 for smooth-parameter conflicts, giving a *computable* limit `lim f_S(N)/N` and thereby certified-optimal tiles for the upper-bound side?
* Does the finite exceptional-set structure of Raouj–Stef–Tenenbaum (2011; "new multiplicative structure for the exceptional set") describe *positive-density* sets of integers with *few* (not zero) close-divisor pairs at scales above a cutoff — which is what an explicit version of idea 1 needs?

Math. 168 (2008) — https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401223 (local `r4/math_0401223.txt`) — REF — Theorem 1(v), Corollaries 1, 3, Theorems 4–5 as quoted in §5.1 — sets the per-window density `(log y)^{-δ}(log log y)^{-3/2}`, `δ = 0.086071`; the profile heuristic is the model for "regularity".
2. Ford, *Integers with a divisor in (y,2y]* — https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607473 (`r3/ford_y2y.txt`) — REF — `H(x,y,2y) ≍ x/((log y)^δ(log log y)^{3/2})`, `3 ≤ y ≤ √x`.
3. Koukoulopoulos, *Localized factorizations of integers*, Proc. LMS 101 (2010) — https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.1072 (`r4/0809.1072.txt`) — REF — Theorem 1: `H^{(k+1)}(x,y,2y) ≍ x/((log y_1)^{Q(1/log ρ)}(log log y_1)^{3/2})`, `ρ = (k+1)^{1/k}`, `Q(u) = u log u − u + 1`; `k=2` exponent `0.2702` — the exact per-scale density of 302 tails/heads.
4. Ford, *Rough integers with a divisor in a given interval*, J. Aust. Math. Soc. — https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02548 (`r4/1901.02548.txt`) — REF — Theorem 1 (§5.4) — shows roughness alone removes only `(log w)^{1/log 2}/log log w`.
5. Maier–Tenenbaum, *On the set of divisors of an integer*, Invent. Math. 76 (1984) 121–128 — via https://www.erdosproblems.com/144 (`r4/ep144.html`) — REF — almost all `n` have `d<d'<2d`, and `<d'(1+(log n)^{-β})` for `β < log 3 −1` — kills every "conflict-free head" construction asymptotically.
6. Erdős–Hall, *The propinquity of divisors*, Bull. LMS 11 (1979) — via #144 — REF — density 0 for `β > log 3 −1`.
7. Tenenbaum, *Some of Erdős' unconventional problems in number theory, thirty-four years later* — https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00488 (`site/tenenbaum1908.txt`) — survey (REF, Bolyai volume) — Stef's `x/(log x)^{β+o(1)} ≪ R_x ≪ x e^{-c√(log_2 x)}`, `β = 0.0041547`; Raouj–Stef–Tenenbaum `E_1(n)` formula; `β_r` values.
8. Stef, *L'ensemble exceptionnel dans la conjecture d'Erdős concernant la proximité des diviseurs*, thèse Nancy 1992 — https://hal.univ-lorraine.fr/tel-01754399 — thesis (unrefereed journal form) — lower bound for `R_x`.
9. Ford–Green–Koukoulopoulos, *Equal sums in random sets and the concentration of divisors*, Invent. Math. 232 (2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00378 — REF — `Δ(n) ≥ (log log n)^{0.35332277…}` p.p.
10. Ford–Koukoulopoulos–Tao, *A lower bound on the mean value of the Erdős–Hooley Delta function* — https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11987 — REF (Proc. LMS) — `Σ Δ(n) ≫ x(log log x)^{1+η−ε}`, `η = 0.3533227`; Koukoulopoulos–Tao upper bound `x(log log x)^{11/4}`.
11. de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum, *Two upper bounds for the Erdős–Hooley Delta-function* — https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13897 — ARX/REF.
12. de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum, *Mean values of arithmetic functions and application to sums of powers*, Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 180 (2025) 1–13 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19320 (`r4/2403.19320.txt`) — REF — Theorem 3.1 (multi-variable Nair–Tenenbaum–Henriot bound) — the three-form engine of Sawin/Della Pietra.
13. Tenenbaum, *Introduction to analytic and probabilistic number theory* (3rd ed.), Thm III.3.5 — BOOK — one-variable mean-value bound used for regularity.
14. Hall–Tenenbaum, *Divisors*, Cambridge Tracts 90 (1988) — BOOK — Ch. 0 Besicovitch/Erdős, Ch. 2 `H(x,y,z)`, Ch. 4 `Δ`, §4.6 `τ^+`, Ch. 5 propinquity.
15. Erdős Problems #144, #446, #448, #449, #450, #693 — https://www.erdosproblems.com/{144,446,448,449,450,693} (`r4/ep*.html`, `r4/tags_divisors.html`) — SITE — statements quoted in §6–§7.
16. Kurlberg–Lagarias–Pomerance, *Product-free sets with high density*, Acta Arith. 155 (2012) 163–173 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5589 (`r4/1107.5589.txt`) — REF — Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.2, Lemma 2.3, Remark 2.7 — `Ω`-window trick; does not transfer.
17. Lichtman–Pomerance, *The Erdős conjecture for primitive sets*, Proc. AMS Ser. B 6 (2019) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02250 — REF — Erdős 1935 `Σ 1/(a log a) ≤ C`; Besicovitch `sup d̄ = 1/2`.
18. Lichtman, *A proof of the Erdős primitive set conjecture*, Forum Math. Pi (2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02384 — REF.
19. Martin–Pomerance, *Primitive sets with large counting functions* — https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.1014 — REF (Acta Arith.) — converse to Erdős's integral criterion.
20. Erdős, *On sequences of integers no one of which divides the product of two others…*, Tomsk 2 (1938) 74–82; Chan–Győri–Sárközy (2010) — REF — `π(n)+Θ(n^{2/3}/(log n)^2)`; `a ∤ bc` sets are 302-free (OWN).
21. Liu–Pach, *The number of multiplicative Sidon sets of integers*, JCTA (2019) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06182 — REF — `π(n)+Θ(n^{3/4}/(log n)^{3/2})` (Erdős 1938/1969) and counting.
22. McNew, *On sets of integers which contain no three terms in geometric progression*, Math. Comp. 84 (2015) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2277 (`r4/1310.2277.txt`) — REF — Theorems 1.4, 3.1, 3.2, 4.1–4.5 as quoted in §8.
23. Nathanson–O'Bryant, *A problem of Rankin on sets without geometric progressions*, Acta Arith. (2015) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2880 (`r4/1408.2880.txt`) — REF — greedy interval sequence `G(k) = ⋃(a_{2i}, a_{2i−1}]`, maximality; Theorem 1: largest primitive subset of `[n]` is `⌊(n+1)/2⌋`.
24. Sawin, *Sets of unit fractions without two members whose average is a unit fraction* — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15419 (`r2/sawin327.txt`) — ARX — orientation trick + same analytic engine (used for idea 5).
25. Della Pietra, Erdős-301 repository — `/home/user/donalddellapietra/erdos-301-proof/paper/main.tex` — unrefereed claim — regularity definition (eq. regular), irregular-count lemma, three-form proposition, exponent `E` — read in §6.4.
